⚑ Headline finding from the grill

The spec's own example numbers can't make pressure rise.

Spec §4 suggests proximity ≈ 0.04/tick and DECAY ≈ 0.05/tick. But adventurers sleep ~8h and drift through private/solo activities — so a pair is only mutually "in public" a fraction of each day. If decay (0.05) outruns gain (0.04) on the majority of ticks, pressure never reaches THRESHOLD and no encounter ever fires.

The fix doubles as the eligibility mechanism. Reframe decay as a constant floor subtracted every awake tick: Δpressure = gain − DECAY. Set DECAY below a public-activity gain but above a private-activity gain. Then a pair drinking together nets positive (builds); a pair where one is off reading nets negative (decays toward 0) — automatically, with no separate binary gate. This is decision D3, and it's the one place we touch the spec.

How pressure is earned each tick
D2

Gain formula — term ranges & commensurability

Spec §4 gives the shape but not the ranges. Three additive terms get summed, then two multipliers scale the sum. For the sum to mean anything the three terms must be the same order of magnitude — otherwise one silently dominates. Here is my proposed range for each, all in "pressure units / tick" against THRESHOLD = 1.0.

gain = ( proximity + moodStrain + relationshipTension ) × compatibilityMult × empathyMult

My recommendation

Adopt the ranges above. The point is proximity is the floor (0.05), moodStrain and relationshipTension are modifiers that can roughly double it under strain — none can dominate. A typical public-activity tick ≈ 0.068 × 1.0 × 1.5 ≈ 0.10/tick; a withdrawn (RESTING/READING) tick falls to ~0.068×0.2 ≈ 0.014, which is the hook D3 uses.

D3

Eligibility & decay — the net-flow model

touches spec

Spec §4 describes two mechanisms that collide: (1) a binary eligibility gate — Private activity → "ineligible" → decay; yet (2) a compatibility table that lists those same Private activities with non-zero multipliers (READING 0.3, RESTING 0.15…), which implies they do accumulate. Both can't be literally true. The spec's own validation test — "a pair kept apart (one always in a Private activity) decays toward 0" — forces a resolution.

// every tick where BOTH are awake & present (not SLEEPING, not questing): Δpressure = gain − DECAY // floored at 0 // SLEEPING or questing → FROZEN (no gain, no decay — sleep is a nightly pause, not a reset) // enemy (≤ −51) w/o crisis, or on cooldown/estrangement → no accumulation

Choosing DECAY between the max private-activity gain (~0.014) and a typical public gain (~0.10) makes the table and the gate the same mechanism: public activity nets positive, withdrawal nets negative. No separate binary flag, and the spec's compat table survives intact.

My recommendation

Adopt net-flow: Δ = gain − DECAY each awake+present tick, frozen on sleep/quest, DECAY = 0.015. Amend social-system.md §4 to drop the binary-eligibility wording in favour of the floor. (Alternative: keep a hard binary gate and zero the Private rows from the table — simpler to test, but discards the spec's graded compat values and the "tension half-life" feel.)

Threshold, discharge & cooldown constants
D4

THRESHOLD & discharge — FIRE_BASE, the 2× cap

primary knob

Once pressure crosses THRESHOLD the encounter is due but does not snap — each tick it rolls a jittered fire chance that rises with overshoot, capped. This spreads the moment of discharge organically across the ticks after threshold instead of firing the instant it's crossed.

fireProb = FIRE_BASE × min( 2, pressure / THRESHOLD ) rng.next() < fireProb → encounter fires this tick
ConstantValueWhy
THRESHOLD1.0normalised; ~10 public ticks of net gain to reach from 0
FIRE_BASE0.2at threshold → 0.2/tick → fires on average ~5 ticks later (geometric)
overshoot cap2.0max fireProb = 0.4; keeps discharge jittered even when wildly overdue (e.g. forced proximity) instead of snapping to near-certain
My recommendation

THRESHOLD=1.0, FIRE_BASE=0.2, cap=2.0. The firing tick is geometrically distributed (memoryless per-tick Bernoulli) with mean ≈ 1/fireProb — that is the organic jitter, no extra random offset needed. The cap at 2× is what stops a hugely-overdue pair from becoming deterministic; without it, jitter collapses.

D5

Cooldowns — post-fire jitter & the ESTRANGEMENT lock

primary knob

On firing, reset pressure to 0 and set a jittered cooldown during which the pair accumulates nothing and can't re-fire — reusing the decisionCooldowns mechanism (CLAUDE.md per-tick-detector rule). ESTRANGEMENT adds its own multi-day approach lock; the effective cooldown is the max of the two.

ConstantValueMeaning
COOLDOWN_BASE8 ticks≥8h before tension can rebuild — blocks same-day re-fire
COOLDOWN_JITTER+0–16tick + 8 + floor(rng·16) → window [8, 24) ticks
ESTRANGEMENT lock120 ticks5 days, both directions, regardless of strength (spec §5)
With ~0.05/day net build to THRESHOLD taking ~2 days, and an 8–24h post-fire lock on top, an average pair cycles roughly once every ~2–2.5 days. Across 10 pairs that lands at ~4–5 encounters/day — the D1 target. The arithmetic closes.
My recommendation

COOLDOWN_BASE=8, COOLDOWN_JITTER=16, ESTRANGEMENT=120. The post-fire cooldown's main job is preventing a single pair from spamming the feed on consecutive ticks; the threshold rebuild time does most of the pacing. This is a top playtest knob if density comes out wrong.
