The Unseen Hand·Event System · Design Discussion

Making events feel alive, not scheduled.

You asked for four things: a terse DnD-narrator voice, events that feel organic instead of clockwork, varying durations instead of fixed hours, and a living town where adventurers interact with more than just each other. This is a map of where the current system fights those goals — and four concrete directions to fix it. Annotate anything; decisions form at the bottom.

Why it feels fake right now

Four root causes, each traced to real code.

Clockwork socialResolver.ts · pairHour()

Every pair talks at the same hour, every day

Each pair is hashed to a fixed hour (0–23). Bree & Cole will always chat at 14:00, forever. That deterministic cadence is the "scheduled and fake" smell.

Instant eventBus · WORLD / SOCIAL

World & social events have zero duration

A storm is one log line, then gone. Only activities have real spans (DURATION_RANGE). Region.activeWorldEvents exists in the type — but is never populated.

Empty town socialResolver · adventurer pairs only

The only people who exist are adventurers

All interaction is adventurer↔adventurer. The "travelling merchant" is a sentence, not someone you can deal with. The town is painted scenery.

Flat voice eventBus · renderText()

Mechanical one-liners for the big beats

"A party sets out on X." The activity/social pools already read nicely — but quest/world/lifecycle text is bare. The LLM only writes a retrospective day summary, never the live feed.

01

Kill the schedule — trigger on pressure, not the clock

The fix isn't randomising the hour — it's changing what causes an event. Events should fire when state builds up enough to demand it, plus a jittered roll, then go quiet on a cooldown. Same average rate; organic texture.

Current

Fixed hashed hour

  • Pair → deterministic hour via string hash.
  • Day-ticks only; one check per pair per day.
  • Repeats identically every day → reads as a timetable.
Proposed

Pressure accumulator + jitter + cooldown

  • Pressure rises from proximity, mood strain, relationship tension.
  • Above threshold, a low per-tick roll decides the exact moment.
  • After firing, a randomised cooldown — no machine-gun repeats.

02

Give events duration — stateful spans, not instants

Activities already do this well. Promote world events (and the weightier social ones) into the same shape: a start tick, a randomised end tick, and effects that hold while active. The home already exists and is empty: Region.activeWorldEvents.

03

The town is alive — nameless NPCs as targets & sources

Adventurers aren't the only people in town. The town runs on two tiers of townsfolk — a small named cast with bios and relationships, plus a deep bench of nameless roles for ambient colour. Both hook off what an adventurer is already doing — someone DRINKING at the inn naturally pulls the innkeeper into a beat — and both deliver world events: the merchant becomes someone, not a sentence.

Tier A

Notable NPCs — named, remembered

A handful per town: the blacksmith, the captain of the guard, the innkeeper. They carry a character bio (name, role, a trait or two) and form relationships with adventurers — favours owed, grudges, a regular they look out for. These are recurring faces with memory.

Tier B

Nameless roles — ambient texture

Everyone else: a shopkeeper, a guard on the gate, an urchin, a drunk. No relationship graph, no mood, no lifecycle — just a role + template pool. Cheap, infinite, and enough to make the town feel populated.

Where notable NPCs live in the model

A bio is small: { id, name, role, traits, bio }. The open question is relationships — do notable NPCs get IDs inside the existing adventurer relationship graph (so bonds, decay, and threshold events reuse all the social machinery), or a parallel NPC↔adventurer map? That's the architectural fork — decided in the form below.

New event shape

An NPC social subtype where one participant is an NPC (named id or bare role), not an AdventurerId. EventFeed needs the usual three updates (KIND_LABELS, ALL_KINDS, getInvolvedIds) per CLAUDE.md — and getInvolvedIds must resolve NPC ids so the character filter works.

04

A narrator's voice — evocative, but spare

DnD-narrator tone is about specificity and restraint, not length. The activity/social pools already nail it. Lift quest/world/lifecycle lines to match, and reserve the LLM for set-pieces — never per-tick (cost, latency, the seeded-determinism rule).

Tier 1 — Template grammar

Deterministic, instant, free. Fragment composition (subject + beat + colour) for variety beyond fixed strings. Carries 95% of the feed.

Tier 2 — LLM set-pieces

Decision moments, quest climaxes, day summary. Already wired (narrator.ts, Haiku). Async, never blocks the tick.

Tier 3 — LLM per-event

Richest, but cost + latency per tick and breaks replay determinism. Likely a no — confirm below.

A sane order to build it

  • Voice
    Lift flat templates → grammar. Lowest risk, instant payoff.
  • Durations
    Stateful world spans in activeWorldEvents.
  • Organic timing
    Replace pairHour with pressure + cooldown.
  • Town & NPCs
    New event subtype; ambient + merchant tie-in.

Each step is spec-first: change specs/behaviors/*, then a plans/ file, then code. Threads are independent — reorder freely.
